Does reincarnation hold consistency under brute-force fault injection? Jepsen's Maelstrom drives the cluster under its fault profile with reincarnation as the failure model: node crashes with volatile-state loss --- the crashed node reincarnates rather than crash-recovers --- plus partitions, with a Maelstrom adapter fronting the lock service as a compare-and-set register and competing lock clients at the lease cadence. The design runs the fault profile at three nodes, then at five, $\mathrm{XXX}$ runs per cluster size. Three checks decide each run: Maelstrom's linearizability check passes over the register --- no committed operation is lost, and no node rejoins under its old identity to overwrite a newer decision; every killed node reincarnates and rejoins serving, with no run ending in a permanently lost node; and no read ever reports two holders, expiry being judged only in the leader's clock. All figures X.XX (medians, milliseconds) and $\mathrm{XXX}$ (run counts). A passing campaign supports the case that reincarnation eliminates the diskless-recovery weakness \cite{diskless}: consistency under crash with successful rejoin, by construction rather than by recovery.

A ballot is the pair $(\mathit{era}, \mathit{view})$ under the lexicographic order; a host may carry it as one scalar wherever the encoding is strictly monotone into a well-founded strict total order, and the agreement proof does not know which representation the wire carried~\cite{artifact}. Under the view's era configuration the leader is a ballot outcome, not a function of membership: \begin{equation*} \ell(v) = \mathit{voters}[\,v \bmod |\mathit{voters}|\,], \end{equation*} the positive-weight sequence in membership order. A boundary that changes that sequence moves the arithmetic mid-plan: at an unmoved view number the new arithmetic may name a different node, and no node can independently compute a stable leader through the transition. The nomination rider settles the assignment at the committed slot itself. The command $\code{Nominate}\{\mathit{from}, u\}$ rides as the last sub-operation of every establishing batch that carries no scaling operation --- a solitary nomination is refused, and a zero offset is refused --- and a node applies it as a compare-and-swap: serving the view $\mathit{from}$, it publishes $\code{Ballot}\{\mathit{era}', \mathit{from} + u\}$, the era component being the era the batch's own fold established, so at a wrap the bump is the era-boundary crossing and no view-change message is owed there. The emission names the least positive offset re-electing the serving leader, \begin{equation*} u = \min\{\,u' > 0 : \ell_{\mathit{next}}(v + u') = \ell_{\mathit{prev}}(v)\,\}. \end{equation*} The rider shares the wrap's establishing slot, so there is no era in which the modulo rule names the wrong leader and none in which the bump has not yet landed. A new leader committing an old leader's nomination finds the compare-and-swap stale and commits it as a no-op. A step that evicts the serving leader cannot re-elect it, and names the era-entering increment alone. The jump's safety licence is the view-jump lemma: a view may jump by more than one during a reconfiguration, because no hypothesis of the agreement argument quantifies over intermediate ballots; eras may not jump, the adjacency discipline of the era map~\cite{artifact}. Scaling boundaries are the remaining case: doubling and halving preserve every entry of the positive-weight sequence, so the leader never moves at one, and the era crossing is the leader-preserving view change to the least view past the running one that selects the same leader. At a moving boundary the parenthesised figure is the rider's offset; at a scaling boundary it is the full voter cycle of the leader-preserving view change.

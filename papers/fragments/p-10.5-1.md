The design claim is that view failover costs $N$ network round trips and \emph{no} forced disk flush, where the classic diskful alternatives pay at least one flush on the fence path. The budget is measured, not assumed: the view change is decomposed into its wire segments and each segment timed. For the three-node cluster the segments are: suspicion (the phi-accrued suspect interval, floored by the host's election knob), \textsc{StartViewChange} dissemination ($1$~RTT), \textsc{DoViewChange} collection at the next view's primary (pipelined with dissemination: $0$ extra RTT when the messages cross), and \textsc{StartView} installation ($1$~RTT): the designed expectation is failover $=$ suspicion floor $+$ $2$~RTT $+$ $0$ flushes. The counterfactual arm pays the same suspicion floor and round trips \emph{plus} a forced flush before fencing (the VSR-1988-class stable-storage barrier) and a further flush before the new primary may serve installed state: suspicion floor $+$ flush $+$ $2$~RTT $+$ flush. Both arms are measured on the same hardware, the same run. The counterfactual is built by the forced-write rig of the campaign plan: a custom test build of the demonstration performs one 4~KiB write and \code{fsync} \emph{outside} the algorithm --- before the fenced node emits its view-change traffic and again before the new primary answers \textsc{StartView} --- so the rig prices the diskful barrier without any uVRR code being made unsafe or rewritten to resemble the compared systems. The reported numbers are the per-segment medians and the totals: suspicion floor X.XX~ms; per-RTT X.XX~ms; total failover, ours, X.XX~ms; total failover, forced-flush arm, XX.X~ms; flush cost per boundary X.XX~ms, expressed also in units of the same-run RTT, X.X~RTTs. The last ratio is the hardware-agnostic headline: it should survive moving between loopback, local virtual machines, and cloud machines, where the absolute latencies will not.

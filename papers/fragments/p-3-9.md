The leader stays live for client requests and drives a forced weight sequence from the voting-weights rules \cite{votingweights}: the exiting node's weight goes $1\to0$ (a Decrement; the Leave follows at weight zero), the new identity joins at weight $0$ (a Join), and the new identity goes $0\to1$. Each step is a unit-weight change, so the strict majorities of consecutive configurations overlap by the unit-change overlap argument and every intermediate era is quorum-safe on its own; a leader dying mid-sequence leaves only safe eras that a new leader can continue. Table~\ref{tab:reincarnationweights} instantiates the sequence at unit weight for the three-voter cluster $A,B,C$ with $B$ the leader and $C$ the crashed identity that returns as $D$: the start configuration is $(1,1,1,\textendash)$; era~1 drives the leader's batch \code{DECREMENT(C)}~+~\code{JOIN(D)}, reaching $(1,1,0,0)$; era~2 drives \code{INCREMENT(D)}~+~\code{LEAVE(C)}, reaching $(1,1,\textendash,1)$. In the table a \textendash{} is not a weight: it marks an identity that is not a member of the configuration in that era, whereas $0$ is the weight of a standby member that the cluster still holds in its order --- which the reincarnated node is between the two eras while it is streamed preemptively. The old identity $C$ is evicted by reincarnation, never a recovered amnesiac. If the sequence runs in the doubled scale of a weight-doubling plan, the return halve is integral only when all weights are even, so the corner sequence inserts one extra unit increment on the new identity before the halve. The leader streams the first reconfiguration, then client traffic, and preemptively streams to the reincarnated node as a weight-0 standby; messages to it are fine and messages from it are discarded by the standard membership check. If the leader crashes, the cluster must reach a stable leader before the reincarnated node forces its old identity's eviction.

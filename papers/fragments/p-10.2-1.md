What does the diskless default save, in latency, against paying a forced flush at the reincarnation boundary? The design builds custom \code{lunet-locks} binaries, identical in every respect other than the durability behaviour at the reincarnation boundary --- the point at which a reincarnating node durably records state before rejoining serving: \begin{itemize} \item \emph{Diskless (the default).} No durable writes at the reincarnation boundary; quorum memory is the only protocol state, and the recovery nonce file remains as shipped. \item \emph{Naive single write (the latency baseline).} One 4~KiB block write followed by \code{fsync} at the reincarnation boundary. Per the FAST'18 finding \cite{par}, stock consensus systems lose committed data under a single storage fault despite \code{fsync}; the variant is the latency baseline only, never a safety claim. \item \emph{Double-ring write.} A TigerBeetle-style double write at the reincarnation boundary: one 4~KiB block of data plus a checksum header to the first WAL ring, then the header and checksum with no payload to the second WAL ring, the two rings spaced apart in separate 4~KiB-sector-aligned zones so the two checksum copies sit in different erasure blocks. Both flush variants carry fake data only, with no read-back: they measure write-and-flush latency and never reconstruct state from what was written. \end{itemize} Each variant runs E1's procedure exactly, $k=\mathrm{XXX}$ iterations per variant, cold counts. The metrics are the kill-to-rejoin-serving distribution per variant (percentiles as in E1) and, for the flush variants, the reincarnation-boundary flush latency itself per iteration. The planned report is the rejoin-latency delta: diskless median X.XX~ms, naive-flush median XX.X~ms, double-ring median XX.X~ms, with tail figures XX.X~ms at the ninth decile. The delta is the utility test: if reincarnation is slower than the forced flush, the diskless design has achieved nothing.

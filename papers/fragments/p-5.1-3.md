$P$ is the invariant, $B$ the guard and $S$ the statement: the same statement carries $P$ from each iteration into the next, which is what makes the repetition one induction rather than a sequence of unrelated steps. The pattern is the Rule of Iteration of Hoare~\cite{hoare1969}, D3 of the axiomatic basis: from $\vdash P \wedge B\ \{S\}\ P$ conclude $\vdash P\ \{\code{while}\ B\ \code{do}\ S\}\ \neg B \wedge P$. The discipline is that of the 1979 lecture: correctness is a mathematical concern, and one precise argument settles an unbounded number of cases~\cite{ewd720}. Two obligations stand apart in the pattern. Preservation is the body's: where $B$ holds and $S$ completes, $P$ holds again. Termination is what supplies $\neg B$ at the exit, and it is a separate proof: an invariant that holds at every step is consistent with a repetition that never ends, so the termination argument stands apart from the preservation argument~\cite{ewd1215}. Here termination is the finite count the envelope carries.

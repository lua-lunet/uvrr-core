The campaign owes an explanation of the detection floor, because the first rig runs showed the phi accumulator firing early: its estimate sat far below the true round-trip time, and under reconfiguration jitter its variance exploded. The runtime is LuaJIT plus FFI driving a Rust core with small messages, so the noise budget is genuinely small; the open question is whether the residual variance is garbage collection, scheduler jitter, or the estimator's window shape, and E6 decides it by attribution rather than by guess. The rig samples, per heartbeat, the measured RTT, the phi estimate, the estimator window's mean and variance, the LuaJIT allocation counter (\code{collectgarbage("count")}) before and after each window, and the Rust-side tick gap distribution; reconfiguration epochs are marked on the same axis. The designed report is the phi estimate against the measured RTT distribution with GC-attributed pauses marked: median estimate X.XX~ms against median RTT X.XX~ms, ninth-decile estimate XX.X~ms against ninth-decile RTT XX.X~ms, GC-attributed share of outlier windows XX.X percent, scheduler-attributed share XX.X percent, unexplained share XX.X percent. The decision rule: if the GC share explains the outlier windows, the estimator gets a variance-capped window and a stated floor; if scheduler jitter dominates, the floor moves to the measured jitter percentile and the paper says which.

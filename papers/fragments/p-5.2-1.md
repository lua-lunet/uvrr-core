A node processes a message through one partial function \begin{equation*} \mathit{message} \times \mathit{state} \to \mathit{state}' \times \mathit{list}(\mathit{messages}), \end{equation*} decomposed as one clause per (node role, message type) pair, with guards on the invariants and an exhaustive match; the sans-IO core of Section~\ref{sec:demo} is this function, made total by its host loop. The fuse is a wire encoding of a schedule over that function, not a new protocol type: $\code{fuse}(c_1, c_2, c_3, \ldots)$ hydrates logically to the phase-2 sequence $\code{phase-2}(c_1), \code{phase-2}(c_2), \code{phase-2}(c_3), \ldots$ at consecutive slots, the envelope carrying the shared ballot once and the first slot once. Read against the construct, the three names instantiate as follows. \begin{itemize} \item $S$ is one message step: the application of the next fused phase two. Each application folds one command through the ordinary accept path at its own slot, exactly as though the commands had arrived as separate datagrams with nothing between them: the per-slot guards --- the next slot is consecutive, the command is legal in the configuration the fold has established, the promise still permits the ballot, the slot's era permits the proposal --- are evaluated against the in-memory state the preceding applications produced. \item $B$ is the loop over the packed slots: the iteration continues while commands of the envelope remain. \item $P$ is the strong-consistency invariant set: the within-era and adjacent-era intersections~\eqref{eq:same}--\eqref{eq:adjacent}, the promise fence of the ballot and the commit frontier's advance, together with the two operational facts the solver is constructed to preserve at every slot of the schedule --- that the leader holds, and that the overlap holds. \end{itemize} The entry assertion~\eqref{eq:loop-entry} is the head guard. The envelope arrives whole or not at all --- one datagram of cache-line scale under the transport's checksum --- so the first command decides the whole batch: when $\code{head.ballot} < \code{state.promise}$ or $\code{head.slot} \le \code{state.commit\_frontier}$, the whole envelope is refused, the loop never runs, and there is no partial acceptance and no negative acknowledgement on the wire. The exit assertion~\eqref{eq:loop-exit} is the tail adoption: on completion the node adopts the tail's ballot as its promise, \begin{equation*} \code{state.promise} \leftarrow \code{tail.ballot}, \end{equation*} one fence covering every packed slot, exactly as if a prepare at that ballot had arrived first: per Lamport, a promise at a ballot covers every lower ballot~\cite{pms}.

Discoverability, addressability, identity, and durability are first-class obligations of the host. Each is stated over happens-before relations of the host's input and output to the network, to the boot fence, and to the durable state: no protocol message leaves before the boot fence records that the process runs, and no announcement names a new identity before that identity is flushed. A cold stop of all replicas followed by a cold start resumes in the same era, because the era is durable in the marker. These relations rest on the behaviour of the storage layer: a single flushed flag is not reliable enough on real file systems~\cite{allfs,optimistic-crash}, which is why the marker is a quorum of copies rather than a flag.

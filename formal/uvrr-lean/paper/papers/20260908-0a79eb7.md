## Introduction

1. The introduction introduces Viewstamped Replication Revisited (VRR-2012)
   before presenting any finding against it. Grade: A (§I ¶1 introduces VSR
   and VRR before the FAST'18 finding in ¶2).
2. The introduction states the FAST'18 crash-recovery amnesia finding with a
   citation to Alagappan et al., Protocol-Aware Recovery for Consensus-Based
   Storage, USENIX FAST 2018. Grade: A (§I ¶2, `\cite{alagappan2018}`, Best
   Paper noted in the references).
3. The introduction states the thesis that Unbounded VSR eliminates the
   amnesia flaw by design: Crash-Recovery is converted to Crash-Stop, and the
   crashed node reincarnates as a new node that rejoins through a
   low-latency reconfiguration. Grade: A (§I ¶3).
4. The introduction states early and explicitly that the paper restates
   Turner's unbounded leader casting-vote techniques in the context of
   Viewstamped Replication Revisited, with full attribution, and that citing
   and crediting a mathematical technique applied to a distinct algorithm is
   valid (Paxos is not VRR). Grade: A (§I ¶4 states the retelling with full
   attribution and the validity sentence "crediting a mathematical technique
   and applying it to a distinct algorithm is valid: Paxos is not VRR").
5. The introduction states what is new beyond Turner's reasoning: the
   executable safety ladder and a working demo, with the
   Crash-Restart-to-Crash-Stop-Reincarnation conversion framed as a failed
   condition removed by design. Grade: A (§I ¶4; "same-identity recovery
   after volatile-state loss is thereby unrepresentable").
6. The introduction points to the experiments section. Grade: A (§I ¶4
   pointer to the experiments section, placeholders and warnings named).
7. The paper's register matches the systems-paper style of the VSR-1988 and
   VRR-2012 introductions; no invented stylistic flourishes. Grade: A (§I ¶1
   follows the VRR-2012 introduction's own sequence).

   attributed contrast, code identifiers flagged as the inherited namespace,

   form) are defined and used consistently. Grade: A (VSR is expanded at its

    Grade: A (§VI.6 names the unit weight changes as a Leave and a Join;

## Formal content

19. The intersection property of integer sets differing by at most one unit
    is presented as trivial (by name, pigeonhole / majority intersection),
    and the real proof burden is identified as leader overlap. Grade: A (§V
    states both: the pigeonhole triviality and the real burden of leader
    overlap across eras).
20. The original contribution is stated as diskless strong consistency
    throughout VRR view changes. Grade: A (§I ¶3 states the contribution at
    stake in those terms).
21. The amnesia flaw is stated precisely: a replica forgets it sent a view
    change message, recovers in a lower view, and committed decisions are
    later overwritten. Grade: A (§VI.4 states the documented pattern in
    exactly those terms).
22. The 3-node degenerate case is stated honestly: its majorities coincide
    and it does not exercise the casting vote; the 2-node case is identified
    as the load-bearing one. Grade: A (§IV.3 states both halves explicitly).
23. Proof-status claims follow the proved-versus-obligation record as of this
    version; obligations are recorded as obligations, not implied as done.
    Grade: A (throughout; correspondence and coverage tables, §VII, §XI).
24. "Load-bearing" is used where the safety argument genuinely depends on a
    component. Grade: A (§IV.3 names the two-node leader-overlap case as the
    load-bearing one).
25. The deferred-flush rationale is stated with its citation: the network is
    faster than the disk; nano-state that fits in memory can defer flushes;
    the network round trips of reincarnation are cheaper than fsyncs. Grade:
    A (§VI.6 and abstract, `\cite{netdisk}`).

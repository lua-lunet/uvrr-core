## Introduction

1. The introduction introduces Viewstamped Replication Revisited (VRR-2012)
   before presenting any finding against it. Grade: A (§I ¶1 introduces VSR
   and VRR before the FAST'18 finding in ¶2).
2. The introduction states the finding that consensus-based storage can
   break the invariants of the very protocol the node runs when a crashed
   node recovers and rejoins as itself, with a citation to Alagappan et al.,
   Protocol-Aware Recovery for Consensus-Based Storage, USENIX FAST 2018.
   Grade: A (§I ¶2, `\cite{alagappan2018}`, Best Paper noted in the
   references).
3. The introduction states the thesis that Unbounded VSR eliminates the
   flaw by design: Crash-Recovery is converted to Crash-Stop, and the
   crashed node reincarnates as a new node that rejoins through a
   low-latency reconfiguration. Grade: A (§I ¶3).
4. The introduction states early and explicitly that the paper restates
   Turner's unbounded leader casting-vote techniques in the context of
   Viewstamped Replication Revisited, with full attribution, and that citing
   and crediting a mathematical technique applied to a distinct algorithm is
   valid (Paxos is not VRR). Grade: A (§I ¶4 states the retelling with full
   attribution and the validity sentence "crediting a mathematical technique
   and applying it to a distinct algorithm is valid: Paxos is not VRR").
5. The introduction states what is new beyond Turner's reasoning: the
   executable safety ladder and a working demo, with the
   Crash-Restart-to-Crash-Stop-Reincarnation conversion framed as a failed
   condition removed by design. Grade: A (§I ¶4; "same-identity recovery
   after volatile-state loss is thereby unrepresentable").
6. The introduction points to the experiments section. Grade: A (§I ¶4
   pointer to the experiments section, all four designed experiments named,
   placeholders and warnings declared).
7. The paper's register matches the systems-paper style of the VSR-1988 and
   VRR-2012 introductions; no invented stylistic flourishes. Grade: A (§I ¶1
